01

Four things to accept first

These four facts shape everything else. Getting them right matters more than squeezing points out of any single category.

SIPs can't be carded

Your ₹80,000/mo mutual-fund SIPs debit the bank via mandate — not a card transaction. ~40% of monthly spend is simply out of scope. Same for maids & domestic help.

Rent earns on exactly one card

Rent is excluded everywhere except Magnus, which earns up to ₹50,000/mo of rent (and it counts toward Magnus's ₹1.5L threshold). A ~1% + GST surcharge applies.

Fuel & utilities earn nothing

Fuel, electricity, gas, WiFi and insurance are excluded on all five cards. Routing them via Amazon Pay doesn't change the category code. Treat as zero-reward.

The real battlefield is ~₹10–11L

Shopping ₹50k, dining ₹20k, groceries ₹10k and travel are the only reward-earning spend. That's the pool this whole system optimises.

⚠ Verify before you buy

Optional: routing dead categories through gift cards

Utilities and insurance earn nothing directly — but an Amazon Pay gift card bought through a voucher portal (HSBC's Gyftr Instant Benefits store, or the CRED gift-card store) usually codes as an ordinary retail purchase, earns your card's base rate, then loads into Amazon Pay balance to clear those bills. Your best shot is HSBC TravelOne at 2 pts/₹100 — Axis reclassifies gift cards to earn nothing from 28 Aug 2026, so Atlas and Magnus are out for this.

Test small before you scale. Buy one low denomination (₹500–1,000) first and confirm all three:

  • The points post — and still survive the next statement cycle. Banks and portals claw back or block gift-card earning without notice, and specific cards get blocked on Gyftr for weeks at a time.
  • You know the portal's monthly cap and your card isn't currently blocked on it.
  • You accept these won't count toward the ₹8L waiver or ₹12L milestone — gift cards are excluded from milestone math, so this is pure base-point earning.

Keep the volume modest. Heavy gift-card buying is a classic flag on the premium accounts you're nurturing toward Burgundy and HSBC Premier — a few thousand rupees a year of points isn't worth risking that relationship.

Massive-month playbook

Expecting a furniture / home-setup blowout? Concentrate it into one statement cycle on Magnus, and let your ₹50k rent ride the same month first — rent fills the low 12/200 bucket, pushing more of the big spend into the 35/200 rate (that ₹3L then earns ~16,400 miles instead of 14,100). Split it across two months and the acceleration never triggers.

Airline miles

Atlas 1:2 and Amex 2:1 both feed Flying Blue & KrisFlyer — the same programs, so pool toward one premium-cabin award. Transfer only when you've found the seat; miles devalue and transfers are one-way.

Hotel points

Amex → Marriott 1:1 is your best hotel exit. HSBC → Accor 1:1 is the only one left in India and often the highest ₹/point. Watch for periodic 20–40% Amex transfer bonuses and time big moves to them.

07

The fuel verdict

Why it's hard

All five of your cards exclude fuel, and Shell has no co-branded card in India — the 5–8% fuel cards are locked to their own IOCL / BPCL / HPCL pumps. The CRED Shell vouchers you spotted (₹500 each, 3/day) are more hassle than the ~₹45/day they'd save. Skip them.

The one clean fix

If ₹1.2L/yr of fuel is worth acting on, a brand-agnostic flat-cashback card (e.g. Kotak Cashback+, ~3% at any pump including Shell) is the only reliable route — worth ~₹3,600/yr. Otherwise, use any card purely for the 1% surcharge waiver and treat fuel as a zero-reward category.

08

Standing watch-outs

  • Devaluation is fast. Axis cut three partners overnight in April 2026. Earn-and-burn beats hoarding.
  • Forex: Magnus is lowest at 2% — use it for general overseas spend. Atlas, HSBC TravelOne and Amex are all 3.5%. (Still book overseas travel on Atlas — its 5x earn outweighs the markup.) For heavy international spend, add a zero-markup card.
  • Acceptance: keep Atlas/HSBC (Visa/MC) as backup; Amex and Diners get declined often.
  • Milestone clocks differ: Amex runs on card-membership anniversary; HSBC/HDFC on their own year. Track each separately.
  • Magnus caps: accelerated earn is limited to credit limit + ₹1.5L/month; rent earning capped at ₹50k/month.
  • Verify before committing: confirm the Magnus rent surcharge and HSBC accelerated cap on a live statement.

The Burgundy flip 🔑

The moment you reach Axis Burgundy, Magnus transfers at 5:4 instead of 5:2. Base earning jumps to 4.8 partner miles/₹100 — beating Atlas even below the ₹1.5L threshold — and that ₹3L month throws off ~28,200 miles. Burgundy turns Magnus from a big-month specialist into your default Axis card. It's the single highest-leverage move in your whole plan.
